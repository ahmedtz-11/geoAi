Natural neighbour takes from each sample the share of ground a new sample at that spot would steal from it: no bull's-eyes, and it never invents values outside your data. Inverse distance is the simple weighted average, predictable but with a bull's-eye at every sample. Ordinary kriging measures how fast the assays decorrelate with distance and weights the samples to minimise the error; it is the only one that also reports its own uncertainty. Minimum curvature gives the smoothest contours but can overshoot in gaps.

Beyond the range two samples tell you nothing about each other — it is roughly the size of the anomalies, and a good guide to infill spacing. The nugget is the share of the variation already present between samples a metre apart: sampling and assay noise, and the nugget effect of coarse gold.

Kriging is the only method here that reports its own uncertainty. The error map is lowest at the samples and rises between them; it depends on where you sampled, not on what the assays were, so it shows where another sample would buy you the most.
